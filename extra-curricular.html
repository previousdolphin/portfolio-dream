Extra Curricular

Beyond the
official record.

Not everything fits a brief. These are the readings, wanderings, obsessions, and side-projects that feed the main work — the life around the portfolio.

Reading

What's on
the desk.

  1. The Control Revolution — James Beniger

    A long argument that information technology has always been about managing futures — quietly central to everything here.

  2. A Pattern Language — Christopher Alexander

    Ostensibly about buildings and towns. Actually about how any designed environment creates or forecloses possibility.

  3. Ways of Seeing — John Berger

    Still the clearest account of how looking is never neutral — essential reading before building anything meant to be seen.

  4. Working in Public — Nadia Eghbal

    On open-source as a social form — applies far beyond code, into any practice that leaves its work visible online.

  5. The Stack — Benjamin Bratton

    An uncomfortable, generative account of the planetary computation we're all embedded in — horizon-expanding in the most literal sense.

Image Making

Looking before
building.

Photography is the practice that precedes everything else — the habit of noticing, of walking to the edge of the frame and looking past it. These images aren't documentation; they're research into what a vista actually requires.

The landscape photographs in this site are working images: taken on location, kept raw, and used here to test how photographs and pages can hold each other up.

Notes

Short thoughts,
long questions.

  • Nov 2024

    On the patience of landscapes

    A vista doesn't arrive. You do. There's something in that inversion that applies to almost every long-form project I've ever worked on.

  • Sep 2024

    The archive as argument

    Every archive makes a claim about what matters. Building The Lot forced me to confront my own assumptions about which failures are worth preserving.

  • Jun 2024

    Typographic whitespace is not empty

    The margins are where the thought happens. A page that breathes isn't wasting space — it's making room for what the reader needs to bring with them.

  • Mar 2024

    What slow web actually means

    It doesn't mean sparse or primitive. It means that every choice was deliberate and every interaction was worth designing. Speed is a default, not a virtue.
